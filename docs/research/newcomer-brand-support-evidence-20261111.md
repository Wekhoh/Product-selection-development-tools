# Q31: evidence of launch support

Checked: 2026-11-11. Scope: public primary documentation and current provider tool descriptions. No competitor records, paid requests, account reports or live market queries were used. All examples below are synthetic. This note clarifies evidence limits; it does not adopt a scoring rule or a finished data contract.

## What an observation establishes

| Observation | Supported conclusion | Unsupported conclusion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Verified earlier products from a seller, or a recognizable brand | The observed seller/product history or brand identity | Customer transfer, promotion spending, common beneficial ownership, or inability to compete |
| An older product's A+ comparison chart or Brand Story links to the new product | A cross-sell route was present at the observation time | Its visits, orders, profit contribution, or necessity for launch success |
| The new product appears in an Amazon recommendation module | An observed placement in that context | The seller deliberately arranged the placement, or every shopper sees it |
| Provider-associated product records, keyword visibility or traffic scores | The coverage or modeled measure defined by that provider | Actual visitor share, order share, advertising spend or organic orders |
| An authorized advertiser report attributes visits or purchases to a source | The report's measured attribution within its stated scope and rules | Public access to a competitor's report, all private marketing activity, or causal incremental sales |

Amazon documents cross-selling through A+ comparison charts and Brand Story links. These establish possible promotion routes, not their effectiveness. Public brand content also does not identify who financed it or establish the legal operator. [Amazon A+ guide](https://sell.amazon.com/blog/a-plus-content-design-guide)

Amazon's Recommended Products tile can select products using the shopper's previous shopping history. A product's presence in a Brand Store is therefore insufficient to identify a deliberate promotion of that specific product; inspect the actual module and context. This official help page describes the mechanism, not a verified current competitor screen. [Recommended Products tiles](https://m.media-amazon.com/images/G/01/AmazonStores/Help/en/Best_Seller_and_Recommended_Products_Tiles.html)

## Provider and account-data boundaries

- SellerSprite's associated-traffic guide explicitly separates associated-product coverage from clicks, visitors and orders. Its free/paid associated-product counts must not be relabeled as transaction shares. Current `traffic_listing_stat`, `traffic_listing`, `traffic_source` and `traffic_keyword_stat` descriptions likewise require preserving the actual measure, including keyword counts where applicable. [SellerSprite official guide](https://www.sellersprite.com/cn/v3/knowledge/feature/relation-keyword-for-beginners)
- SIF's public MCP documentation describes traffic scores, observed rankings and exposure-based contribution. Current `ops_get_listing_traffic_overview` metadata defines channel scores and ratios relative to their parent, not verified order shares. Single-ASIN and listing scopes differ. A missing channel is not zero; missing crawler rankings can reflect incomplete coverage. Metadata review does not validate actual coverage or returned values. [SIF official MCP documentation](https://www.sif.com/mcp)
- Brand Store source insights are accessed through the advertiser's account. Amazon Attribution requires product/tag setup and authorized reporting access. These are not public competitor order-source lookup facilities. Customer lists, private messages, unobserved marketing and competitor source-specific conversion remain unknown without relevant authorized evidence. [Brand Store source insights](https://advertising.amazon.com/resources/whats-new/enhanced-brand-store-sources-insights), [Amazon Attribution](https://advertising.amazon.com/solutions/products/amazon-attribution)
- Even authorized reports use attribution rules. The Store help documentation distinguishes direct/halo sales and assigns sources using a last-discovery method. Inference: an attributed purchase does not by itself prove the purchase would disappear without that exposure. Verify the applicable report's current definitions before comparing results. [Store attribution definitions](https://m.media-amazon.com/images/G/01/AmazonStores/Help/en/Stores_Insights/Orders_sales_and_units.html)

## Concrete synthetic examples

1. **An established seller launches a countertop organizer.** Earlier unrelated products are verified, but no specific promotion route is observed. Public searches cannot certify that private support does not exist.
2. **An older product links to the new organizer in its A+ comparison chart.** Record the source product, destination, module and observation date: a published cross-sell route is confirmed. Its order contribution and the newcomer's dependence on it remain unknown. It does not establish that 80% of visitors or orders came from older products. A separate traffic-score percentage would still need its own definition, period, scope and coverage; it would not become verified order attribution.
