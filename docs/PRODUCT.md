## Discovery and analysis

Start with measured keyword and market data rather than a fixed list of imagined words. Investigate stable demand, growth and supported supply gaps. Communities and other marketplaces may discover new directions or corroborate pain points; popularity alone does not prove purchase demand or low competition.

The inherited discovery baseline includes five connected routes. This table makes the existing breadth explicit; it does not introduce new score dimensions or require every route for every candidate.

| Route | Where a new direction can originate | Follow-through |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Keyword libraries and demand records, the main route | Measured popular, rising, changing or persistent purchase searches, including specific attribute/use queries | Check historical demand, buying intent and matching offers; a native sustained-growth mode is only one retrieval option. |
| Product databases, newcomer lists and market records | Observed physical forms and newcomers with sales evidence; relevant BSR and New Releases records | Define the comparable niche, inspect actual launch history and reverse lookup purchase entries. |
| Sourced competitor and public store research | A source-backed product, its related keywords, adjacent forms and publicly observable store assortment | Expand to distinct needs or forms, preserving the original source and avoiding cosmetic duplicates. |
| People, tasks, use contexts and recurring review problems | An evidenced user's task, repeated inconvenience or offer mismatch, including adjacent uses and attributes | Find searchable buying expressions and existing solutions; a complaint does not prove an unsolved or feasible improvement. |
| Social and cross-platform discovery | Specific products, buying requests, repeated problems or changing interests on X, Reddit, Pinterest and other public platforms | Social evidence can originate a direction before an Amazon keyword or product is known, as well as corroborate an existing candidate. Return to Amazon to verify purchase demand and comparable competition. |

SellerSprite and SIF are intended research inputs within their verified capabilities; do not assume either provider supplies every route or field. Google Trends can support interest, seasonality and event checks, but its relative index is not Amazon purchases. A ranking is a lead, not proof of demand, low competition or a viable product. Broad-category and newcomer lists require direct-niche and real-launch checks.

Evaluate growth, stable demand and supply-gap opportunities across these routes. Test apparent growth against incomplete periods, low bases, events and seasonality; stable markets can contain accessible niches; a supply gap needs purchase-demand and offer-mismatch evidence rather than mere lack of listings. Review and social signals must lead back to Amazon demand, substitutability, economics and rights checks. A repeat pain point does not prove that a feasible light improvement exists.
