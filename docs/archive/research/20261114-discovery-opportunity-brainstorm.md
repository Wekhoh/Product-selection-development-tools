## Question and working recommendation

How can discovery identify more distinct, economically plausible opportunities for a small seller using available products or feasible light changes, without simply copying popular keyword lists or requiring difficult invention?

The working recommendation is to vary the questions asked of evidence, not just the number of searches. A data provider is an observation channel, not a selection strategy. Shared data can support different decisions about buyer tasks, useful configurations, surrounding purchases and economics. A less visible product is not automatically a better opportunity.

## Assumptions worth challenging

- **A longstanding complaint must be impossible for a small seller to address.** Sometimes it reflects a hard performance, safety or manufacturing problem; defer that direction. Sometimes an existing solution is offered in the wrong size, quantity or context. Investigate which explanation fits before proposing development. Neither large-firm inaction nor repeated complaints establish a feasible gap.
- **High social attention means every related product is already crowded.** Attention to an activity and competition for each supporting product are different observations. Inspect the adjacent purchases rather than apply a new social-heat cutoff.
- **Low search volume for one phrase means nobody knows how to buy the product.** Buyers may describe the same purchase through several uses or attributes. Group observed purchase intentions before deciding; do not sum overlapping terms as independent demand or invent keywords as measured evidence.
- **Historical portfolio concern, explicitly reserved to the owner on 2026-11-15:** products sharing a fad, use or season can share risk, but portfolio selection is the owner's responsibility. Do not use this concern to suppress, deprioritize or limit otherwise supported cards. Multiple substantive forms in one market may all be delivered; merge exact duplicate evidence rather than impose diversity quotas.
- **Historical suggestion, explicitly deferred by the owner on 2026-11-15:** a small market's launch expenses and ongoing attention might matter to the later investment decision. The owner directs initial selection to retain modest-volume products with supported demand and suitable unit economics, and to leave photography, sampling and portfolio maintenance to their later work. Do not use these overhead considerations to reject or deprioritize initial cards. No minimum-sales cutoff or new product-margin cost follows; see the dated decision in DECISIONS.
- **Every attractive quality deserves another score.** A scene problem or emotional motivation may already support demand evidence and need fit. Record the specific causal claim before deciding whether anything new needs points; no extra bonus is adopted here.

These are reasoning hypotheses, not quantified causal findings or new exclusions.

Activity-related purchases, adjacent uses and product clusters are concrete uses of already retained methods, not newly invented routes. Recurring workplace purchases, purchase convenience and explicit emotional motivations are proposed emphases to evaluate. Cross-platform discovery already existed; the useful additions are a clear transfer hypothesis, image-led vocabulary recovery and their failure checks.

## A compact investigation example

Suppose independent sources show adults increasingly doing a particular craft. Do not jump from that observation to selling the most prominent kit. Examine existing buyers' related purchases: storage, work surfaces, carrying equipment, display and replenishment. A ready-made organizer might already solve one task; a complicated new mechanism might not be necessary.

Then look for Amazon purchase expressions, actual competing offers and their price/cost structure. If buyers already have a cheap adequate substitute, reject the improvement story. If the proposed set only reaches the price floor by adding unwanted pieces, reject that configuration. If a source merely shows attractive pictures, retain it as a discovery lead until purchase evidence exists. No claim is made that this illustrative craft or accessory is currently attractive.

Two founder interviews provide useful historical counterexamples. Relevant transcript passages were inspected by the research worker and independently fetched/read back by the lead. Reliable publication/update dates were not established; these are not represented as recent 2025/2026 cases or current cost benchmarks:

- [Lars Hundley, Clean Air Gardening](https://mywifequitherjob.com/clean-air-gardening-lars-hundley/) describes selling existing manual mowers, expanding into related gardening tasks and later replacing an unavailable compost bin. He reports roughly $80,000 in tooling for that replacement. The lesson is conditional: an established product becoming hard to obtain can expose demand, but the apparently obvious substitute can require resources far beyond a ready-stock strategy. Investigate an available simpler alternative rather than assume cheap manufacture. His account is firsthand and self-reported, not independently audited.
- [Steve Chou, customer-discovery interview](https://unofficialshopifypodcast.com/episodes/steve-chous-boneheaded-mistakes/) describes discovering that handkerchief buyers included collectors and crafters, not just wedding customers; he changed positioning using observed customers and post-purchase answers. He also describes repeat professional buyers receiving dedicated service. This supports investigating unexpected users and professional configurations. It does not establish that their demand, direct-service advantage or profitability transfers to an Amazon listing. Public usage accounts can start hypotheses but are weaker than actual customer purchase evidence.

No sufficiently established recent firsthand seller case was found in this bounded pass. Current official platform observations and older practitioner mechanisms are kept separate; neither is a representative success-rate sample.

## Proposed next experiment, after discussion

Keep the existing scoring framework separate from discovery-method evaluation. Once trials resume, investigate suitable leads from the available lenses, including steady practical tasks and activity-related or cross-platform opportunities. The owner's 2026-11-15 clarification permits multiple cards from the same market, use or trend; do not impose a diversity quota. Reuse market observations where they apply. Do not require every candidate to traverse all platforms, reconstruct every historical exit or undergo final supplier/patent due diligence before an initial card.

Use the existing candidate record to state the originating observation, specific purchasable form, buyer reason, Amazon purchase evidence, initial economic basis and strongest contrary explanation. Record rejection reasons and time to useful scored cards. Evaluate whether a method produces supported, separately assessable candidate forms; thematic diversity is not required. More words or source links alone do not establish a better result. A trial score is not a calibrated probability of commercial success.

No new fixed quotas, automatic self-adjustment, paid integration, supplier contact or recurring job follows from this recommendation.
